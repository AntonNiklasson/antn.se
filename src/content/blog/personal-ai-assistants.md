---
title: Personal AI assistants
date: 2026-11-25
---

I have been experimenting with personal AI assistants recently. OpenClaw kicked
it off earlier this year with a bang. It was such a step change from what I had
tried before. It was actually mind-blowing. It felt like a long-time dream of
having my own Jarvis was coming to fruition.

But looking back at it now, it feels almost ahead of its time. I did not trust
the foundation models as much as I do today, so it felt like there was always a
risk of something going south. After this summer though (I heard someone refer
to it as the post-Opus 5.5 era) it is all different. Models are now basically
always doing what I want, and very rarely something I do not want. If does go
wrong, it is usually due to me vague prompting in some way or underspecifying
what is not desired. I spend most of my time directing and steering, and very
little time correcting, tweaking, or redoing.

This led me to try Instinct, and most recently Grok Bot. I have also tried to
use ChatGPT extensively as a general assistant, but some varied success. I am
happy to hand over my email and calendar to these tools. If possible I am also
hooking them up with my Todoist setup for tasks, and Obsidian for notes.

## Instinct

Instinct has been great. It is basically a bot I can message any time over
iMessage, and it seems to rely heavily on browser use and great context
management to help me accomplish anything I ask for. I have had it order sushi
for me, reach out to nearby vendors for car services, research products and
monitor blocket.se, and continuously clean out my email inbox. Just various
mundane, normal life tasks, basically.

The main drawback for me is that it is essentially single-threaded, even though
you could manage everything through the reply feature in iMessage. Having
different conversations, or even bots, feels cleaner to me. It is easier to
manage. I am also missing key integrations, like Todoist and Obsidian in my
case. I like how they solved the vault though, and I appreciate the
capapbilities it has around generating artifacts, like a custom little website
for me when I did some research on what desk chair to buy for my son.

## ChatGPT

ChatGPT is great as a whole. Lots of integrations available, but it is not
really built as an assistant. It is more of a chat product with integrations. It
is a bit weak on the proactive angle. I am excited to try Dots, but it is not in
Sweden yet, as far as I am aware.

I have been experimenting a bit with scheduled tasks here, but it easily gets
overwhelming. Being able to spin up a new thread per thought basically is nice,
but it lacks coherence and a feeling of a continuous conversation I can come
back to any time.

## Grok Bot

Grok Bot is the most recent product I am trying. It is from SpaceX AI, which I
am honestly not really a fan of. But their products are undeniably interesting.
It is available through their X subscription, which includes some kind of bundle
of Cursor, X, Grok (which are the actual models), and Grok Bot (the assistant
product).

The bot product leans heavily into this idea of employing a bunch of AI
coworkers, and it really feels that way when you start using it. Each bot has a
personality, and you are encouraged to assign them a job or a role. I am
currently running one as a kind of CFO. One is my digital janitor, helping me
clean out inboxes and unsubscribe from unwanted newsletters. Another is for
general research, and one is focused on family-related organisation.

They all read my emails, schedule things in my calendar, and remember different
things we have talked about. Most importantly, they also take more and more
notes through the native Obsidian integration Obsidian now offers. They also
capture tasks in Todoist and help me make sure to pick up what is due today in
the morning.

The biggest question for me right now is pricing. I am tempted to build
something like this just for myself, but that would have to be more of a
response to privacy concerns rather than cutting costs. Getting this all right
the way they have would take me a long time, if I ever even could.

Overall, I feel like there is a ton of interesting innovation happening in this
space every week. I am excited to see more and more proactive solutions enter
the market. There is a lot of magic yet to be built there.
